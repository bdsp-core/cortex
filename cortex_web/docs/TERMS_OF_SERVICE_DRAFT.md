## `[counsel]` decision points (must be finalized before publishing)

1. **Limitation of liability — cap.** The public draft excludes indirect/
   consequential damages and limits liability "to the maximum extent permitted
   by law," but states **no monetary cap**. `[counsel]` to decide whether to add
   a specific cap (e.g. the greater of fees paid or US$100) and any carve-outs,
   and to confirm enforceability against international consumers.
2. **Dispute resolution / venue.** The public draft chooses California law and
   exclusive jurisdiction of California state/federal courts. `[counsel]` to
   decide whether to (a) add **binding arbitration + class-action waiver**,
   (b) specify a county/venue (e.g. Santa Clara County), and (c) include
   non-waivable-rights carve-outs for EU/UK/other consumers.
3. **Indemnification.** **No user-indemnification clause is included.** For a
   free educational/research tool aimed at clinicians this was judged
   non-essential and potentially over-reaching; `[counsel]` to decide whether to
   add a narrow indemnity for a user's misuse, IP violation, or unlawful use.
4. **Provider legal entity name.** Public copy uses "Stanford University School
   of Medicine (Westover Lab — CORTEX project)"; Stanford's formal legal entity
   is **"The Leland Stanford Junior University."** `[counsel]` to confirm the
   correct contracting entity (same open item as the Privacy Policy).
5. **Export controls / sanctions & international use.** `[counsel]` to confirm
   whether an export-control / restricted-jurisdiction clause is needed given a
   worldwide clinician audience.
6. **Click-through assent.** Signup currently states "by creating an account you
   agree to … the consent terms." `[counsel]`/team to decide whether an explicit
   "I agree to the Terms of Service and Privacy Policy" checkbox is required for
   enforceable assent.

## Risks / notes
- The ToS leans on the **separate IRB research consent** for study participation
  and on the **Privacy Policy** for data handling — keep all three consistent if
  any one changes.
- "No medical advice / not a credential" language is deliberately strong to
  manage clinical-reliance risk; keep it if results are ever shown as scores.
- IP section asserts Stanford/Lab ownership of the methods and a separate
  data-use-agreement regime for the EEG corpus; confirm this matches the actual
